# Hyperbolic crochet — Daina Taimiņa and the Institute For Figuring

These are core visual references for the `mostow` project. The point is not merely decorative: crocheted hyperbolic models make the intrinsic growth of the hyperbolic plane tactile and visible in a way that the Poincaré disk and upper-half-plane coordinate models do not.

- Second edition: *Crocheting Adventures with Hyperbolic Planes: Tactile Mathematics, Art and Craft for All to Explore*, CRC Press, 2018.

The construction to keep in mind is simple: increase the number of stitches repeatedly as one moves outward. The fabric acquires more circumference than a Euclidean sheet can accommodate and consequently ruffles and crenellates. It supplies a physical model whose intrinsic geometry can be explored by touch.

## Design consequence for `mostow`

Do not treat the Poincaré disk or upper half-plane as *the shape* of the hyperbolic plane. They are coordinate/conformal models.

For interaction, distinguish:

1. the intrinsic hyperbolic metric;
2. a coordinate model such as the disk or upper half-plane;
3. a ruffled three-dimensional physical visualization in the spirit of Taimiņa's crochet;
4. quotient/gluing views, where boundary pieces are identified and the glued locus can remain visible as a bright seam.

The three-dimensional ruffled surface is an extrinsic visualization, not a unique embedding of the complete hyperbolic plane in Euclidean 3-space.
